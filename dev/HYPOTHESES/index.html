    Research questions and hypotheses¶

    The benchmark compares ways of obtaining a long synthetic time series: assembly from a pool of short windows (different ways of selecting and ordering windows and of processing seams) and long-series generators. The questions below are posed about families of methods and about the benchmark itself. The quality of synthetic data is measured in two ways: fidelity (how closely the series resembles the real one in its statistics, F1–F12, against three real references — train, val and OOT) and utility — TSTR versus TRTR: a model trained only on synthetic data (TSTR — train on synthetic, test on real) is compared with the same model trained on real data (TRTR — train on real, test on real), on the same real test rows of val and OOT, in classification and regression tasks. Metrics are described in METRICS.md.

    The benchmark result consists of four parts:

    1. Overall leaderboard (marathonts/hypotheses/overall.py → artifacts/<profile>/reports/OVERALL.md, §3, RQ1, Overall leaderboard) — one ranking of every method by fidelity on the core metrics (against train, val and OOT) and utility (classification and regression), with noise-aware ranks and robustness checks; privacy and cost are not part of it.
    2. Ranking (RQ1, marathonts/hypotheses/leaderboard.py → artifacts/<profile>/reports/LEADERBOARD.md) — the rank of each method in each slice: fidelity × reference (train / val / OOT), utility × task family × split (val / OOT), cost; with confidence intervals and tests; four rankings — fidelity against val + OOT or against train × utility classification or regression; privacy (F10) and cost are ranked separately and are not part of them.
    3. Research questions RQ0–RQ9 (marathonts/hypotheses/questions.py) — conclusions with statistical tests; the confirmatory family C1–C4 (§3.9) gives the main conclusion of the paper.
    4. Case study CS1–CS15 (marathonts/hypotheses/catalog.py, §4) — one method under study (flag --case-method, by default vae_zseries) against its controls and alternatives. Secondary; any method can be analysed this way.

    The overall leaderboard heads artifacts/<profile>/reports/REPORT.md; everything else goes into it too (<profile> — min, mid or max; default min). The values of the metrics behind the rankings — artifacts/<profile>/reports/METRIC_VALUES.md (marathonts/hypotheses/metric_values.py): values of the core fidelity metrics and the primary utility metrics by reference, dataset, task, model and split.

    1. Methods and roles¶

    Method Family Role in the analysis
    vae_rand assembly: random selection the simplest assembly from the VAE pool: K windows in random order; reference point for RQ2–RQ3
    vae_rand_b assembly: random selection a second random order of the same pool — noise (the difference between two equally random assemblies); mid and max only; not ranked, serves as a check (RQ0, RQ1)
    vae_zseries, vae_greedy, vae_atsp, vae_pctsp, vae_mbb, vae_knn, vae_quilting, vae_herding assembly: optimised selection different ways of selecting and ordering windows from the same pool (BASELINES.md, section A); vae_zseries — zseries, the proprietary assembler of zypl.ai (under development): its results are published (Config.frozen_methods), its code is not part of the repository
    vae_zseries_{xfade,wsola,quilt} assembly: seam processing crossfade, WSOLA, quilting on top of the vae_zseries path (section B; published results; max: in addition on top of every assembler except the random path and vae_quilting)
    diffusion_ts long-series generator Diffusion-TS, autoregressive extension (trained once per dataset in min / mid, on each seed in max)
    chronos long-series generator Chronos, trajectory sampling without training
    timesfm, timestral long-series generator TimesFM 3.0 and Timestral: the median forecast after train, without training; the same series on every seed: no between-seed spread of their own, so in RQ2 the margin is the spread of the compared method (§2)
    real reference real OOT data (real-data ceiling for fidelity, RQ0) or a model trained on the real train (TRTR, utility reference)

    A method's family is determined from its description (bench.methods: pool + assembler + seam processing, or a long-series generator), so custom methods (EXTENDING.md) enter the ranking and the questions automatically.

    2. General testing rules¶

    Direction of the effect. For a pair "A versus B" the difference d = orientation × (A − B) is computed, where the orientation is +1 for "higher is better" metrics and −1 for "lower is better" metrics. d > 0 always means "A is better than B". Which method plays the role of A is stated in each question (for example, in RQ2 A is the long-series generator, in RQ3 A is a way of selecting windows or of processing seams).

    Units of replication. - Within a dataset — seeds (10). A seed determines generator training, the pool, the random paths and the models. Methods with the same seed form a pair (and a block in the ranking). - Across datasets — datasets (9). The global conclusion is drawn from dataset means, not from pooled seeds; otherwise large datasets would not be independent.

    What a seed changes in different profiles. In max, VAE and Diffusion-TS are retrained on each seed: the per-dataset conclusion generalises over the training procedure. In min, the VAE is trained once per dataset, and seeds change only pool sampling and random paths: the per-dataset conclusion is conditional on this trained model, and the between-seed spread (the noise for comparisons with independently trained methods) does not include training variability and is therefore smaller; Diffusion-TS in min is also trained once per dataset. In mid the VAE is retrained on every seed (seeds are independent and include training variability) and Diffusion-TS is one model per dataset as in min: its between-seed spread covers sampling only; diffusion_train_replicas (or dts_train_per_seed) adds training variability to it. Comparisons on a single pool (RQ3, CS1–CS8) are not affected by this: all assembly methods use the same pool. The global conclusion over the 9 datasets generalises over datasets in any profile.

    Tasks a synthetic series could not train. A TSTR cell (task × model × split) can be missing for a method whose series exists: its labels have one class (a smooth median forecast has no move above the threshold), it gives no rows, or the fit failed. Left out, such a cell would rank the method only where it succeeded — on the easier datasets. With Config.utility_fail_penalty = True (default; --no-fail-penalty turns it off) the cell enters as a model that learned nothing (hypotheses.catalog.impute_failed_utility, column imputed). Each metric takes the worse of two values: the chance level — ROC AUC 0.5, PR AUC = positive share p of the test rows, log-loss H(p) for a binary task and ln K for a K-class task, Brier p(1 − p) — and the worst value any method has in that block (dataset, seed, task, model, split); a metric without a chance level — regression ones and the metrics of plugin tasks — takes the worst value of the block. An imputed cell therefore never ranks above a method whose series trained the task. The chance level is the score of a constant prediction, so AUC is not set below 0.5: an AUC below 0.5 is an inverted ranking, not an absence of skill. The expected cells are those of TRTR on the same (dataset, seed), so the rule covers every method, task and model, built-in or plugin. A method left out on a dataset for leakage (Config.leaked) is not imputed there, and neither is a method with no unit at all. The raw tables in artifacts/<profile>/tables/ are not changed.

    Noise and the smallest effect size of interest. For each (dataset, metric) pair, the noise is the median of |vae_rand − vae_rand_b| over seeds: how much two equally random assemblies of one pool typically differ within one run. In min, where vae_rand_b is not run, it is the median of |vae_rand(seed k) − vae_rand(seed k+1)|: the pool is resampled on every seed, so this noise also contains the pool's sampling variance and is wider — the verdicts are more conservative. This is the pre-specified smallest effect size of interest (SESOI): it serves both as the threshold of practical significance ("better" only if the effect is larger) and as the equivalence margin ("the same" if the effect is shown to be smaller). The median rather than the 95th percentile: with 10 seeds the 95th percentile is close to the maximum, and "the same" would be obtained almost always. Noise is a property of the pool, not of any method.

    For comparisons with independently trained methods (long-series generators in RQ2, C2), the noise is the median of |A(seed k) − A(seed k+1)| — the between-seed spread, including generator retraining — of whichever of the two compared methods has the larger one. A pair of random assemblies of one pool does not have this component, and a generator used without sampling (TimesFM, Timestral: the same series on every seed) has none of its own, so the comparator's spread sets the margin.

    Global effects are expressed in noise units (z = d / noise), so datasets and metrics of different scales are comparable.

    Verdict for each unit of analysis (a metric, or task × model × regime × split):

    Verdict Condition
    better the one-sided Wilcoxon "A is better" is significant after correction at level α/2, and the effect exceeds the noise (per dataset — the Hodges–Lehmann estimate, globally — median z > 1)
    worse the same in the opposite direction
    same equivalence is demonstrated: TOST (two one-sided Wilcoxon tests) with margin ±noise is significant
    inconclusive none of the conditions: the data are insufficient to say "better", "worse" or "the same"

    "Same" is demonstrated absence of a difference, not absence of significance. Absence of significance is inconclusive.

    The verdict can go either way, so each one-sided p is compared with α/2: together this is a two-sided test at level α, and the probability of a false directional conclusion does not exceed α. TOST is tested at level α. If the noise of a metric on a dataset is zero (a discrete metric, all values coincide), the smallest effect size of interest is undefined: the per-dataset verdict is inconclusive, the dataset is excluded from the global test, and the number of such datasets is shown in the column n_datasets_zero_margin.

    Tests.

    Level Test Purpose
    dataset, over seeds Wilcoxon signed-rank (one-sided in both directions), TOST, Hodges–Lehmann, rank-biserial non-parametric paired test: metric distributions over seeds are not normal
    global, over datasets Wilcoxon on dataset means, sign test (share of datasets where better), t-test on dataset means, two-level bootstrap (datasets → seeds) for the confidence interval, TOST with margin ±1 noise unit several tests with different assumptions; the conclusion is robust if they agree. The unit is the dataset: seeds of one dataset are not independent
    many methods (ranking, RQ0, C1) Friedman (blocks — datasets); each method against the best — one-sided Wilcoxon over datasets with Holm (Benavoli et al., 2016); Nemenyi CD — for reference whether there are any differences at all, and who is statistically indistinguishable from the best. The Nemenyi CD grows with the number of methods, and its conclusion depends on the set of methods
    ranks two-level bootstrap of ranks (datasets → seeds), probability of being first / in the top 3 uncertainty of the position in the ranking
    ranking agreement (RQ1, RQ5) Kendall τ whether the order of methods coincides across slices
    relations between metrics (RQ0, RQ4) Spearman within a dataset across methods, Wilcoxon over datasets, BH which metrics duplicate each other, which predict utility
    over test rows (CS7, CS12, CS13) predictions are averaged over seeds, ΔAUC on the same test rows — block bootstrap by weeks the difference is visible on the test itself. Not DeLong per seed: rows are autocorrelated (labels at horizon h overlap), and DeLong gives false positives several times more often than nominal; the seeds share one test
    cost median time with bootstrap over datasets, slope of log(time) on log(K) how much a method costs and how time grows with series length

    Multiple comparisons. Three levels. - Confirmatory — the family C1–C4 (§3.9): two pre-specified cells (classification and regression), Holm correction over all tests of the family together; a missing test enters with p = 1 and receives the verdict missing, so the correction is not weakened. The result is the first section of REPORT.md and confirmatory.parquet. Only these give the main conclusion of the paper. The cells and the composition of the family are fixed (§3.9). - Secondary (RQ0, RQ2–RQ6, case study): Benjamini–Hochberg within a question. The conclusion is "where better, where worse", with false discovery rate control. - Exploratory (RQ7, CS11, CS12 subgroups, CS15): p-values are shown, but conclusions are formulated as hypotheses for future work.

    The correction is applied at two levels: global p-values — within a question, over all units of analysis and comparisons; p-values of per-dataset verdicts — within each "question × dataset" pair, by the same method. The number of datasets with each verdict in the global table is counted from the corrected verdicts.

    Power. With 10 seeds the minimal one-sided Wilcoxon p ≈ 0.001, so a verdict on a single dataset is possible. With 9 datasets the minimal one-sided p ≈ 0.002. A global conclusion over dozens of metrics after correction will be obtained only for large and consistent effects. An effect visible on 2–3 datasets remains inconclusive globally and is visible in the per-dataset verdicts. The main lever of power is the number of datasets, not of seeds.

    3. Research questions¶

    RQ0. Validity of the benchmark¶

    Question. Does the benchmark measure what it should? (a) Two random assemblies of one pool (vae_rand_b versus vae_rand; mid and max only) are equivalent on all metrics. (b) Real OOT data are better than the methods in fidelity. (c) Which metrics discriminate between methods beyond the noise at all. (d) Which metrics duplicate each other, and how much the conclusion depends on the reference.

    How it is tested. - (a) The noise is estimated on even seeds and TOST on odd seeds, so as not to measure noise and equivalence on the same data (noise_floor). - (b) The share of datasets where real is better than the method, for each metric (ceiling_share_real_better). The real OOT is shorter than the synthetic series (20% of the series versus K·T), so each method is evaluated on the first len(OOT) points of its series (<name>@len). A metric is flagged as uninformative (uninformative_metrics) if the median of this share over methods is below 0.5. - (c) For each metric (fidelity against train / val / OOT; TSTR utility for each cell) — Friedman across methods, blocks — datasets, BH; plus the ratio of the spread of methods (IQR of the methods' means over seeds) to the noise, median over datasets. A metric is discriminative if p_BH < α and the spread > 1 noise unit. Summary — the share of discriminative metrics by group and reference. - (d) Spearman between metrics over (dataset, method) pairs after ranking the methods within a dataset: pairs with |ρ| ≥ 0.9 (redundant_pairs) and the agreement of one metric against different references (reference_agreement: ρ val ↔ train, val ↔ OOT).

    Supports: that the verdicts of the other questions are calibrated, and shows which metrics to rely on. Does not support: anything about the quality of the methods.

    RQ1. Ranking of all methods in all slices¶

    Question. How do the methods rank in each slice and in each of the four rankings, and with what uncertainty?

    Ranked methods. Every method of the tables, the published results of frozen_methods included, except the second path of the noise pair and — unless every assembler has them — the seam-processing variants (*_xfade, *_wsola, *_quilt). Seam processing runs over vae_zseries only, so ranking its variants would let vae_zseries enter four times and every other assembler once. With smooth_paths="all" (max) every assembler but the random path has every seam processing and the variants are ranked. vae_quilting (choice of windows and boundary cut together) is one method, not a variant, and is always ranked. Config.rank_exclude / --rank-exclude sets the list explicitly (no names — rank every method). The methods left out of the rankings take part in every other question and in the case study.

    Slices (leaves). Fidelity — one metric against one real reference: train, val or OOT (suffix @train / @oot, no suffix — val; group F10 — distance to train — is computed once, slice "—"). Utility — TSTR of one task × model × split (val / OOT) × metric. Cost — time (total, training, generation) and memory.

    Ranks. Within a leaf, methods are ranked for each (dataset, seed) pair — methods of one seed share the pool and the test rows and form a block; rank 1 is the best, ties receive the average rank; if a method is missing from a block, the ranks of the others are rescaled to 1…k (a TSTR cell the method's series could not train is not missing: it enters as a model that learned nothing, see §2). Ranks are averaged along a fixed hierarchy with equal weights at each level, so that a group with dozens of metrics does not outweigh a group with two:

    leaf (metric | task × model × metric) → group (F-group | task type) → criterion × slice (reference / split)
     → criterion (mean over its slices) → domain (fidelity | utility) → ranking
    Criterion Groups Slices
    fidelity: distribution F1, F2, F12 (outliers and extremes) train, val, OOT
    fidelity: dependence F3, F4 (without calendar F4.4–F4.5: for Chronos, TimesFM and Timestral the calendar is given) train, val, OOT
    fidelity: windows F6, F9 train, val, OOT
    fidelity: long_structure F7 train, val, OOT
    fidelity: target F8, F11 (class balance) train, val, OOT
    fidelity: seams F5 train, val, OOT
    fidelity: privacy F10 —
    utility: classification U2.1–U2.5 (clf., multiclass) val, OOT
    utility: regression U2.6–U2.9 (regression, quantiles, multi-step, volatility) val, OOT
    cost time and memory of the series total (total time), training (generator training), generation (sampling + assembly of a new series by the trained model), memory (peak GPU memory and RSS increase)

    Thematic slices — separate rankings by cross-cutting properties; leaves are taken from different groups, each metric has equal weight, and they do not enter the rankings (their leaves are already in the criteria):

    Theme Fidelity (train / val / OOT) Utility (val / OOT)
    class balance F8.1, F11.1–F11.4 ECE of binary classifiers: a shift of the class share in the synthetic data gives poor calibration on real data
    outliers and extremes F1.5 (q01, q99), F1.6 (kurtosis), F1.10 (Hill), F2.4, F7.4, F12.1–F12.5 regression error on the extreme 10% of the target (mae_extreme), PR AUC of rare events

    A utility criterion includes the primary metric of the task type (AUC, log-loss, MASE, pinball) and the models trained on all tasks of the criterion (in min / mid — linear and boosting; the MLP, trained on one task only, is kept as a separate leaf).

    Rankings (leaderboard.RANKINGS). Four rankings, each the mean of two domains with equal weight: fidelity — the mean of the six fidelity criteria (distribution, dependence, windows, long_structure, target, seams), each averaged over its references; utility — one criterion, TSTR on val and OOT.

    Ranking Fidelity Utility
    holdout_clf val, OOT classification (val, OOT)
    holdout_reg val, OOT regression (val, OOT)
    train_clf train classification (val, OOT)
    train_reg train regression (val, OOT)

    Within a fidelity criterion the references are averaged equally. Not part of the four rankings: privacy (F10, computed once against train; its own table — the criterion fidelity:privacy@—), cost (time and memory), the thematic slices. Cost is reported next to each ranking: the same ranking with cost as a third domain of equal weight (total time and peak memory; with_cost:<ranking>) and the Pareto front "rank × median time" (on the front: no other method has both a lower-or-equal rank and a lower-or-equal time, one of them strictly lower). Training of a shared generator (the pool VAE, Diffusion-TS in min / mid) is charged to each method and seed that uses it; times from different hardware are not comparable. Every table in LEADERBOARD.md is preceded by its composition and the meaning of each of its columns.

    Core-metric rankings (leaderboard.CORE_RANKINGS, core_<ranking>). The same four rankings with the fidelity domain restricted to a core set of one or two canonical metrics per criterion (leaderboard.CORE_METRICS); utility is unchanged. The core rankings give the order on a small set of metrics with a defined interpretation; the full rankings, over every fidelity leaf, are reported next to them.

    Criterion Core metrics
    distribution F1.2 Wasserstein of values, F2.1 KS of 24 h changes
    dependence F3.5 log power spectrum (every lag), F4.1 weekly profile
    windows F6.1 discriminative score, F6.4 MMD of windows
    long structure F7.1 and F7.2 KS of the rolling mean and rolling spread
    target F11.1 class shares of every classification task
    seams F5.7 detectability of the seams

    The set is fixed in advance by what the metrics measure, not by how well they separated the methods in a run: choosing the metrics that discriminate (RQ0 (c)) on the same data as the ranking would select noise that happens to favour some method and inflate the differences.

    Normalised scores (normalized_summary.parquet, normalized.parquet). Each core metric on the scale random assembly (noise_pair[0]) = 0, real data = 1: (x − x_random) / (x_real − x_random); above 1 — closer to the reference than the real data, below 0 — worse than a random assembly. Fidelity: against val and train, on the series cut to the length of the real reference (<method>@len, seed 0), the same length as real; utility: TSTR against TRTR, seeds averaged within a dataset, models fitted on every task. A dataset is left out of a metric when its anchors differ by no more than the noise. Summary: median over the metrics of a criterion within a dataset, then over datasets. The scores give the size of the effect next to the ranks and do not enter the rankings.

    Anchor check (anchor_check.parquet). For every fidelity metric (against val and train) and every utility cell: per dataset, gap = |real − random assembly| and noise = median over seeds of |random − twin|; ratio = gap / noise, median over datasets; separated — ratio > 1, i.e. the metric tells real data from a random assembly beyond the noise. The competing methods are not used, so the check does not depend on who wins. Computed on all seeds and separately on even and odd seeds: a flip — the verdict differs between the two halves. Fidelity: real is a piece of OOT, shorter than the synthetic series, so for distance metrics the gap is underestimated and the check is conservative. The check is a diagnostic of the metrics; it does not change the core set.

    Domains (leaderboard.DOMAINS) — fidelity or utility ranks as a whole, alongside the criteria: fidelity against train, val, OOT and over all three (without F10), utility on val, OOT and on both.

    Uncertainty and tests. Two-level bootstrap (datasets, then seeds within a dataset) of mean ranks → rank interval, probability of being first and of entering the top 3. Friedman with blocks — datasets. The top tier is the best method and all methods that are not significantly worse than it (one-sided Wilcoxon over datasets, Holm). Place and tier. The place of a method is its position by mean rank; the tier is a test against the best. The tests do not separate the places within the tier, and these places do not reproduce between runs on different hardware (BENCHMARK.md, "Published runs"), so the leaderboard is read by tier. Tier membership is not monotone in the place: a method that loses to the best on almost every dataset leaves the tier even with a close mean rank, while a method with variable ranks, or one ranked on fewer datasets because of leakage, can stay in it. Sensitivity to weights: each ranking under 2000 random criterion weights (Dirichlet(1) within each domain) — share of first places, top 3, and Kendall τ with equal weights. Noise check (mid, max): vae_rand and vae_rand_b, ranked together with all others, must be indistinguishable in each criterion — two-sided Wilcoxon over datasets for the difference of their ranks, Holm over criteria (twin.parquet). The Nemenyi CD is reported for reference only: with a dozen or more methods it exceeds the rank differences this check tests.

    Result: artifacts/<profile>/reports/LEADERBOARD.md and the tables in artifacts/<profile>/leaderboard/ — rankings.parquet (the four rankings), rankings_summary.parquet (rank, P(first) and tier in each ranking), rankings_cost.parquet (rank with cost, median time and the Pareto front for each ranking), by_column.parquet (each ranking, ranking with cost, domain, criterion × slice with intervals), by_group.parquet, leaves.parquet (each leaf), tests.parquet, stability.parquet, kendall.parquet, weights.parquet, transfer_ratio.parquet, twin.parquet (mid, max); core-metric rankings — rankings_core.parquet, rankings_core_summary.parquet; normalized.parquet, normalized_summary.parquet, anchor_check.parquet.

    Overall leaderboard (marathonts/hypotheses/overall.py). One ranking, its composition fixed in advance. Fidelity — the core criteria (CORE_METRICS), each the mean of its ranks against train, val and OOT with equal weights: the aim is synthetic data close to the real series, and the three parts are three pieces of it (train — the longest and most precise reference; val and OOT — not seen by the generator); the split exists for utility, so that TSTR has no leakage. Utility — TSTR classification and regression with equal weights, each tested on val and OOT. Overall = the mean of the two domains. Privacy (F10) and cost are not part of it.

    Noise-aware ranks. Within a leaf (dataset, seed, metric × reference or task × model × metric × split) two methods whose scores differ by no more than the noise of the leaf tie: rank = 1 + #{methods better beyond the noise} + ½ #{other methods within the noise}; with zero noise this is the ordinary average rank. Noise of a (dataset, leaf) — the estimate of the anchor check: median over seeds of |vae_rand − vae_rand_b|, or, without the twin (min), of the between-seed difference of vae_rand. Between seeds the pool changes too, so the estimate bounds the within-seed noise from above and the ties are conservative. Near-identical methods then share a place instead of splitting the ranks of the others. Ranks are averaged as in the four rankings; uncertainty and the tier — two-level bootstrap, one-sided Wilcoxon against the first, Holm.

    Next to the rank. Fidelity and utility ranks alone and their Pareto front; normalised scores (random assembly = 0, real data = 1; criteria and domains averaged with the same equal weights; fidelity against val and train — real is a piece of OOT); a memorisation flag — the share of synthetic windows closer to train than the 5th percentile of real-to-real distances (F10.2) against the same share of the real OOT piece: flagged when the median ratio over datasets is ≥ 2 and the excess is significant (one-sided Wilcoxon over datasets, Holm over the methods). The flag does not change the rank; F10 does not tell copying from a collapse onto typical windows.

    Robustness. Sensitivity variants fixed in advance: ordinary average ranks; fidelity against train only; against val + OOT only; without the metrics with no anchor check (F5.7 — real data have no seams; F6.1 against val — not separated from the noise); every fidelity metric instead of the core set; only the datasets where every method is ranked — each with Kendall τ against the leaderboard. One method per generator: each method ranked in a field of one method per generator (the random assembly represents the VAE pool), so that many variants of one generator cannot move the others. Leave-one-dataset-out: best and worst place. Random criterion weights: Dirichlet(1) within each domain, 2000 draws.

    Result: artifacts/<profile>/reports/OVERALL.md and the tables in artifacts/<profile>/overall/ — overall.parquet (the leaderboard with the columns next to the rank), criteria.parquet, variants.parquet, sensitivity.parquet, field.parquet, lodo.parquet, weights.parquet.

    RQ2. Assembly from a window pool versus long-series generators¶

    Question. How does assembly from a pool of short windows compare with generating a long series as a whole?

    Comparisons. Each long-series generator (A: diffusion_ts, chronos, timesfm, timestral, custom ones) against (1) the simplest assembly — the random path vae_rand, and (2) the assembly median — the median of all pool assembly methods for each (dataset, seed, metric), the pseudo-method assembly_median. The median rather than the best assembly method: the best method would be selected on the same data on which the comparison is made.

    Metrics: utility — TSTR on the primary metric of each task × model × split; fidelity — all metrics against val and OOT. Noise — the larger of the between-seed spreads of generator A and of the assembly it is compared with (§2). BH over the whole question.

    Supports: where assembly from windows is better than, worse than or equivalent to holistic generation — by task type, metric group and over time (val / OOT).

    RQ3. Within assembly: window selection × seam processing¶

    Question. What matters more when assembling from one pool: how windows are selected and ordered, or how seams are processed?

    Comparisons (all on one pool — noise vae_rand vs vae_rand_b, in min the between-seed spread of vae_rand). With several pool generators (e.g. pool_suites={"vae": …, "gan": …}) RQ3, C3, C4 and the assembly median of RQ2 / C2 use the pool of noise_pair only; the other pool's methods are ranked (RQ1, overall leaderboard, normalised against their own pool's random assembly) and the analysis warns at start. Run the analysis with --noise-pair gan_rand gan_rand_b for the contrasts within the other pool: - (a) each selection method (zseries, greedy, atsp, pctsp, mbb, knn, quilting, herding) against the random path vae_rand; - (b) each seam processing against the same path without processing (*_xfade, *_wsola, *_quilt against vae_zseries; in max against every assembler); - (c) the main effect of each processing (processing − no processing, averaged over the assemblers that have every processing: vae_zseries; in max every assembler except the random path).

    The random path has no seam processing, so there is no factorial contrast "selection × processing" with a random level. In min and mid (b) and (c) rest on the published results of vae_zseries, whose code is not part of the repository (§3.9).

    Metrics: as in RQ2. BH over the whole question. The smallest effect size of interest is the same for all comparisons and contrasts — the noise of §2: it is a threshold of practical significance, not an estimate of the contrast's variance (for main effects, which are means over several methods, the variance is smaller).

    Supports: the contribution of each assembly factor. Comparing a specific method with its controls is the case study (§4).

    RQ4. Does fidelity predict utility¶

    Question. Which fidelity metrics — and against which reference — tell us which synthetic data will train models better?

    How it is tested. For each fidelity metric (oriented: higher is better; against train, val and OOT) and each utility criterion (transfer ratio of a task family on val or OOT; median over seeds, tasks and models of the criterion), Spearman ρ across methods is computed within a dataset. Over datasets: median ρ, share of datasets with ρ > 0, Wilcoxon of ρ against 0, BH over all "metric × criterion" pairs. A metric is predictive if p_BH < α and median ρ > 0.

    Result: a table of metrics with ρ; a summary by reference (by_reference: whether fidelity against OOT predicts better than against val or train) and by metric group (by_group), the top 5 predictors of each criterion. In the ranking the same is visible as Kendall τ between the fidelity and utility criteria.

    Supports: which metrics to use for selecting generators without training models. Does not support: causality — correlation over ~16 methods per dataset.

    RQ5. Gap to real data and robustness¶

    Question. (a) How does each method's TSTR compare with TRTR? (b) Is the ranking stable over time (val → OOT) and across model classes (linear ↔ boosting)? (c) Do decisions made on synthetic data transfer to real data?

    How it is tested. - (a) Transfer ratio by method × criterion × split: median over datasets, bootstrap interval over datasets, one-sided Wilcoxon (ratio − 1 < 0) over datasets, BH. Verdict worse than real or inconclusive. - (b) Kendall τ between the val ↔ OOT and model ↔ model (linear ↔ gbdt; every pair of models fitted on every task of a criterion) rankings (from the ranking) and the rank shift OOT − val of each method. - (c) Only the max profile (decision_transfer=True): Spearman between the ranking of a grid of 11 model configurations on real data and on the method's synthetic data (U6.1), threshold transfer (U6.4).

    RQ6. Speed and cost¶

    Question. How fast is each method — in training and in generating a new series — how much memory does it need, and which methods lie on the Pareto front "quality × time"?

    How it is tested. Median total time over datasets with a bootstrap interval, time relative to the fastest, slope of log(time) on log(K) with bootstrap over datasets (how time grows with series length); cost components — generator training, generation of a new series by the trained model (sampling + assembly), peak GPU memory, RSS increase (cost_parts); cost ranks over these slices with the tests from the ranking (cost_ranks); for each of the four rankings of RQ1 the Pareto front "rank × time" and the rank with cost as a third domain (pareto). Generation is separated from training because in practice a generator is trained once, while series are generated many times. Times from different hardware are not comparable — the report warns about this.

    RQ7. Moderators (exploratory)¶

    Question. How do the effects of method families vary with dataset properties?

    Test: Spearman between dataset descriptors (ACF at 24 and 168, kurtosis of increments, volatility clustering, trend, share of missing values) and the dataset effects from RQ2 and the main effects of RQ3 (median over metrics, in noise units), BH. With 9 datasets — exploratory only. Needs the prepared data (data/processed, for the descriptors) and at least 4 datasets with an effect; without the data RQ7 and CS15 are skipped with a message, the rest of the report is unaffected.

    RQ8. Class balance¶

    Question. Do the methods preserve class shares in all classification tasks — above all for rare events — and how much does an error in the share cost for models trained on synthetic data?

    How it is tested. - (a) Ranking on the "class balance" theme (RQ1) with Friedman tests and Wilcoxon against the best. - (b) Family comparisons on the theme's metrics (F8.1, F11 against val and OOT; TSTR ECE of binary and multiclass tasks where computed): each assembly method against the random path (pool noise) and each long-series generator against the assembly median (between-seed spread of the generator); BH over the question. - (c) Relation: within a dataset, Spearman across methods between each balance metric and the theme's utility (mean ECE rank), Wilcoxon over datasets, BH — whether a wrong class share explains poor TSTR calibration.

    Why separately. A random assembly of windows preserves the class share only on average; any method may shift class shares or under-represent rare events (F11.4). In fidelity aggregates such shifts are averaged with dozens of other metrics.

    RQ9. Outliers and extremes¶

    Question. Do the methods preserve the tails, outliers and extremes of the real series, and does this matter for models that must predict extremes?

    How it is tested. As RQ8, on the metrics of the "outliers and extremes" theme: fidelity — tail quantiles, kurtosis, Hill index, large jumps, outlier durations, F12 (frequency and magnitude of exceedances of the real 0.5% / 99.5%, local spikes, weekly maxima and minima); utility — regression error on the extreme 10% of the target and PR AUC of rare events. (a) Theme ranking, (b) family comparisons, (c) relation of fidelity tail metrics to quality on extremes.

    Why separately. Generation and seam processing can change the tails of the series and the spikes at window boundaries; metrics over the whole distribution are little affected by such changes.

    3.9. Confirmatory family C1–C4¶

    Two pre-specified cells: classification — U2.1 (upper tail, h = 24, q = 0.90), AUC, and regression — U2.6 (h = 24), MASE; both — TSTR, linear model, OOT (questions.CONFIRMATORY_CELLS). In each cell:

    Test What it tests How
    C1 whether the methods differ at all Friedman over the methods of the default ranking (not changed by --rank-exclude), blocks — datasets; a method left out on a dataset (Config.leaked) drops out of the test, the dataset stays
    C2 diffusion_ts and chronos against the assembly median as RQ2, noise — the larger of the two between-seed spreads; the composition is fixed (CONFIRMATORY_GENERATORS): custom long-series generators are compared in RQ2 but do not enter the family
    C3 main effect of window selection the mean of every selection strategy of the default ranking (no seam processing) against the random path
    C4 main effect of seam processing all processings against no processing, averaged over the assemblers that have every processing (vae_zseries; max: every assembler but the random path)

    In total 10 tests (2 + 4 + 2 + 2) with a single Holm correction on the two-sided p. Verdict: C1 — differ / inconclusive; C2–C4 — better / worse if p_Holm < α and |median z| > 1, same if TOST (Holm) is significant.

    In min and mid the seam processing runs over vae_zseries only (smooth_paths=["zseries"]), whose code is not part of the repository (frozen_methods). C4 and RQ3 (b, c) therefore rest on its published results: they can be checked from the tables, not recomputed. The seam processings themselves are open; smooth_paths=["greedy"] (or any open assembler) computes them over an open path — an addition outside the pre-specified protocol, not a replacement of C4.

    4. Case study: one method against its controls¶

    The questions of §3 compare families. The case study answers a question about one method — by default vae_zseries (Config.case_method, flag --case-method): how it compares with its controls on the same pool (vae_rand), other assemblers of the same pool and long-series generators, and what drives it. Below, "the method under study" is this method; the sign of the effect d > 0 means it is better than the method it is compared with. The case study is secondary (BH within a hypothesis; the pre-specified cell CS7 + CS10 — Holm within it) and does not enter the confirmatory conclusion of the benchmark. vae_zseries is zseries, the proprietary assembler of zypl.ai (under development): the case study reports its results against its controls; its code is not part of the repository (Config.frozen_methods). The benchmark evaluates the quality of synthetic data (TSTR versus TRTR), not the benefit of augmentation.

    CS1. Distribution and dependence against train¶

    Hypothesis. vae_zseries is better than vae_rand on the metrics of the marginal distribution, increments and temporal dependence measured against train — the data the pool generator was trained on.

    Metrics: the metrics of F1–F3 against train (@train), except F3.2 (it belongs to CS4).

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; BH.

    Supports: that the method changes the distribution and dependence of the series relative to a random assembly of the same pool. Does not support: realism on unseen data (this is CS2) or usefulness for models (CS7, CS8, CS13).

    CS2. Distribution and dependence against val¶

    Hypothesis. vae_zseries is better than vae_rand on the same metrics measured against val.

    Metrics: the metrics of F1–F3 against val, except F3.2.

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; BH.

    Supports: that what the method changes against train holds on unseen data. Read together with CS1. Does not support: usefulness for models (this is CS7, CS8, CS13); realism of long structure (CS4) and of seams (CS3).

    CS3. Seam artefacts¶

    Hypothesis. The seams of vae_zseries are less noticeable than those of a random assembly.

    Metrics: F5.1–F5.7. Checks: F5.4 (local ACF), F5.5–F5.6 (a change-point detector finds the seams), F5.7 (a classifier distinguishes a segment containing a seam from a random one).

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; BH. In addition, in CS9 and CS10 the seams are compared with the chunks of the generation of Diffusion-TS, Chronos and Timestral (there a "seam" is a chunk boundary).

    Supports: that the method changes the seam metrics relative to a random assembly. Does not support: that the seams are indistinguishable from real transitions: for that, F5.7 should be about 0.5, while the comparison with vae_rand only says "better than random".

    CS4. Structure longer than a window¶

    Hypothesis. vae_zseries is better than vae_rand on properties at scales larger than the window length T.

    Metrics: F3.2 (ACF at lags T…3T), F6.4 on 2T windows (MMD of windows spanning a seam), F7.1–F7.7 (rolling statistics, level crossings, outlier durations, drawdowns, trend, regimes).

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; BH.

    Supports: a difference between assembly and random assembly of short windows in the long structure of the series. Does not support: that the long structure is better than that of a long-series generator (this is CS9).

    CS5. Target structure¶

    Hypothesis. vae_zseries is better than vae_rand on the metrics of the target: class shares and event dynamics.

    Metrics: F8.1 (class shares), F8.2 (event duration), F8.3 (time between events), F8.4 (events by hour of the week), F8.5 (conditional probability of an event given features), F8.6 (conditional distribution of the future change).

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; BH.

    Supports: that the method changes the target structure of the series relative to a random assembly of the same pool. Does not support: usefulness for a classifier (this is CS7).

    CS6. Assembly does not change the realism of individual windows and does not increase memorisation¶

    Hypothesis (equivalence). vae_zseries and vae_rand do not differ on metrics of individual windows of length T and on memorisation.

    Metrics: F6.1, F6.2, F6.4 (T windows), F6.6, F6.8, F9.1–F9.2, F10.1–F10.2.

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; expected verdict "same" (TOST); BH.

    Why. Assembly does not change the content of windows; it only selects and orders them. Hence, on metrics of individual windows it should coincide with a random assembly. If assembly is "better" on them, it systematically selects more typical windows; F9.1 (recall, coverage) then shows whether diversity decreases.

    Supports: that the effects of CS2–CS4, if any, are obtained by ordering and selection, without loss of diversity and without copying train. Does not support: that VAE windows are realistic in an absolute sense — this is a property of the generator, visible in CS9.

    CS7. TSTR against a random assembly — pre-specified cell of the case study¶

    Hypothesis. On task U2.1 (stitch_task), a model trained only on vae_zseries synthetic data performs better on real data than one trained only on vae_rand synthetic data. TRTR is common to all methods (the same real data, the same model seed), so "higher TSTR" and "closer to TRTR" are the same thing.

    Metrics: task U2.1 (upper tail, h = 24, q = 0.90), ROC AUC — the task of the case-study cells (stitch_task); it carries no extra weight in the benchmark. The pre-specified cell of the case study is the linear model, OOT (Holm together with CS10, case_cell.parquet); the other models and val are secondary. It does not enter the benchmark's confirmatory family (§3.9).

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; noise — a pair of random assemblies of one pool (min: the between-seed spread of vae_rand); Holm; block bootstrap over test rows.

    Supports: that it is the assembly, and not the VAE pool itself (vae_rand), that brings the synthetic data closer to real data.

    Does not support: transfer to other tasks (CS8) and models (CS11).

    CS8. TSTR on the other tasks¶

    Hypothesis. TSTR of vae_zseries is higher than TSTR of vae_rand on tasks U2.2–U2.9: other horizons, rare events, lower tail, multiclass, regression, quantiles, multi-step forecasting, volatility.

    Metrics: the primary metric of the task — AUC (binary), log-loss (multiclass), MASE (regression, multi-step forecasting, volatility), pinball (quantiles). All models and both splits.

    Comparison and test: against vae_rand; BH.

    Supports: that assembly brings the whole series closer to the real one across tasks. clf_down_h24_q10 is the opposite tail.

    Does not support: that the effect is the same across tasks. The per-task verdicts are the answer to "where better, where worse".

    CS9. Fidelity against other assemblers and long-series generators¶

    Hypothesis (two-sided). On which metrics the method under study is better than, worse than or equivalent to (a) other ways of assembling a series from the same VAE pool — sections A and B of BASELINES.md: vae_greedy, vae_atsp, vae_pctsp, vae_mbb, vae_knn, vae_quilting, vae_herding, vae_zseries_{xfade,wsola,quilt}; (b) the long-series generators (baselines, by default all four: Diffusion-TS, Chronos, TimesFM, Timestral).

    Metrics: all F-metrics against val except F4.4–F4.5: calendar coherence is given by construction for Chronos, TimesFM and Timestral (the calendar is extended by time), whereas for VAE and Diffusion-TS it is generated, so these two metrics measure different things for the two groups and are left out.

    Comparison and test: against each method of (a) — the noise of a pair of random assemblies (the same pool); against (b) — the between-seed spread of the method under study; BH over all comparisons of the hypothesis together. The result is read by metric group (F1…F12) and by dataset.

    Supports: a "where better / where worse" map relative to all alternatives. The comparison with vae_herding separates the effect of window selection from that of their order; with vae_zseries_{xfade,wsola,quilt} — what seam processing adds on top of the method's own path.

    Does not support: that a difference from (b) is due to assembly rather than the VAE. This is separated by the pair CS9 + CS2/CS4: if vae_rand also differs from the baseline in the same direction, the difference is attributed to the generator, not to the assembler.

    CS10. TSTR against other assemblers and long-series generators¶

    Hypothesis (two-sided). On which tasks and models the TSTR of the method under study is higher than, lower than or equivalent to the TSTR of (a) other assemblers of the same pool (as in CS9) and (b) the long-series generators (as in CS9).

    Metrics: primary metrics of all tasks, regime S. Test: against (a) — the noise of a pair of random assemblies, against (b) — the between-seed spread of the method under study; BH over all comparisons together. The cell "U2.1, linear model, OOT, AUC" against Diffusion-TS and Chronos (fixed in advance, PRESPECIFIED_LONG) enters the pre-specified cell of the case study (Holm together with CS7).

    Supports: a practical comparison: whose synthetic data are more useful to models.

    Does not support: the reason for the differences. This is shown by the comparison with CS7: if vae_rand also scores below the baseline, the difference is attributed to the VAE generator, not to the assembler.

    CS11. The effect of assembly on TSTR does not depend on the model class (exploratory)¶

    Hypothesis. The effect of assembly (S vae_zseries − S vae_rand) has the same sign and a comparable size for the linear model, boosting and MLP.

    Test: Friedman across models with datasets as blocks: tasks, splits and seeds within a dataset are averaged (they share a series and are not independent as separate blocks); before averaging, the effect of each task is divided by a single number — the median |effect| of that task over all models — so that tasks of different scales are comparable and the order of models does not change. Share of blocks where the sign of the effect coincides for all models. Only tasks on which all models are trained enter: in min and mid the MLP is trained only on U2.1, so there CS11 is about U2.1; in max — all 14 tasks (without XGBoost the fallback boosting has no multi-step task, which then drops out).

    Supports: that the effect is not an artefact of one model.

    Does not support: transfer to models outside the set (sequential networks, MiniRocket).

    CS12. The effect is stable over time and across subgroups¶

    Hypothesis. (a) The effect on the TSTR of the case-study task (U2.1) is the same on val and on OOT (the shift in time does not destroy it). (b) The effect is not concentrated in one subgroup of test rows: weekends / weekdays, calm / medium / turbulent periods (volatility terciles).

    Test: (a) for each dataset, the shift of the effect d_OOT − d_val (mean over seeds) is divided by the median of the same shift for the noise pair (vae_rand − vae_rand_b) — this is also a difference of differences, with the same variance (min, without the twin: the between-seed spread of the shift of vae_rand); TOST ±1 over datasets, against vae_rand and every long-series generator (baselines); BH. (b) ΔAUC within subgroups over rows (regime S), Wilcoxon over datasets, BH — exploratory; requires data/processed (calendar and volatility of the test rows), skipped without it.

    Supports: that the conclusion of CS7 is not an accident of one period or market regime.

    Does not support: stability at horizons beyond OOT.

    CS13. TSTR of the method under study against TRTR¶

    Hypothesis (two-sided). A model trained only on vae_zseries synthetic data (TSTR, regime S) is worse than, better than or as good as a model trained on the real train (TRTR, regime R) on real val / OOT.

    Metrics: the primary metric of each task (AUC, log-loss, MASE, pinball), all models and both splits. In addition — the transfer ratio of each method (TSTR relative to TRTR; 1 means the synthetic data train the model as well as real data): for AUC — (TSTR − 0.5) / (TRTR − 0.5), the share of quality above chance; for other "higher is better" metrics — TSTR / TRTR; for losses — TRTR / TSTR. Tables CS13_transfer_ratio_case_cell (pre-specified cell by dataset) and CS13_transfer_ratio_by_task.

    Comparison and test: vae_zseries in regime S against real in regime R; general procedure (noise — the between-seed spread of the method under study); BH. Over test rows — block bootstrap of ΔAUC by weeks for seed-averaged predictions (U2.1).

    Supports: how close the synthetic data are to real data in terms of information useful to the model: a verdict of same means TSTR is equivalent to TRTR within the margin for this task and model.

    Does not support: that the closeness is due to assembly — this is CS7 / CS8; who is closest to TRTR — RQ1.

    CS14. Computational cost¶

    Hypothesis (two-sided). (a) The total time of the method under study (VAE training + pool sampling + assembly) is lower than, higher than or the same as (±10%) that of every other method (the random path, the alternative assemblers, the long-series generators), and how much the assembly itself adds compared with a random assembly. (b) Assembly time grows polynomially with K; the exponent is estimated.

    Metrics: fit_wall_s, sample_wall_s, assemble_wall_s, total_wall_s, peak GPU memory and RAM — recorded by the benchmark for each run together with the hardware.

    Test: Wilcoxon on the log ratio of times (over datasets), TOST ±10%, bootstrap interval of the ratio; slope of log(time) on log(K) with bootstrap over datasets.

    Supports: the cost of quality and scalability to long series (PJM, K = 519). Does not support: anything if times were measured on different hardware — the analysis issues a warning when the table contains more than one GPU type.

    CS15. Moderators: where assembly is better or worse (exploratory)¶

    Hypothesis. The size of the assembly effect (CS2, CS4, CS7, CS10) is related to dataset properties: daily (ACF 24) and weekly (ACF 168) seasonality, heaviness of the increment tails, volatility clustering, trend, length K, share of missing values.

    Test: Spearman correlation between the dataset effect (in noise units) and a descriptor, BH. With 9 datasets — exploratory only.

    Supports: hypotheses about which series show a larger or smaller assembly effect. Does not support: a causal relationship — 9 points and many correlated descriptors.

    6. How to read the result¶

    1. Confirmatory conclusion (REPORT.md, after the overall leaderboard) — the main conclusion of the paper: whether the methods differ (C1), how assembly from windows compares with long-series generators (C2), what window selection (C3) and seam processing (C4) contribute — in classification and regression.
    2. Overall leaderboard (OVERALL.md, first section of REPORT.md) — one order of the methods by fidelity and utility, the top tier and how stable it is under the sensitivity variants, a field of one method per generator, dataset removal and random weights.
    3. Ranking (LEADERBOARD.md) — the order of methods in each slice, the top tier, rank intervals; how stable the ranking is with respect to the reference, split, model and weights. Metric values (METRIC_VALUES.md) — the measured values behind the ranks: the core fidelity metrics and the primary utility metrics by reference, dataset, task, model and split, TRTR (real) for scale.
    4. Questions RQ0–RQ9 — a map of "where better, where worse, where the same" by method family with false discovery rate control; RQ0 — which metrics can be relied on, RQ4 — which of them predict utility.
    5. Case study — secondary: where one method differs from its controls and alternatives.

    All four reports are in artifacts/<profile>/reports/.

    Inconclusive is not "no effect" but "the effect is smaller than the noise or inconsistent across datasets". "Same" is demonstrated equivalence.

    7. Pipeline¶

    The analysis (marathonts/hypotheses/) runs on the benchmark tables (BENCHMARK.md). It does not generate data or train generators: it needs artifacts/<profile>/tables/*.parquet, and the case study also needs the utility predictions and labels (artifacts/<profile>/utility, artifacts/<profile>/labels); dataset descriptors (RQ7, CS15) and the CS12 subgroups read data/processed (without it these parts are skipped). It runs on CPU. The per-dataset tests of all comparisons run as matrices (the exact sign-flip permutation test that scipy's Wilcoxon uses for tied or zero differences with ≤ 13 seeds is computed in one matrix product, with the same p-values), and every bootstrap draws all its resamples at once.

    Data flow¶

    What is required from the benchmark¶

    • All methods of the profile on the same seeds (in the tables: 16 in min — 12 run by the pipeline and the 4 published results of frozen_methods —, 17 in mid, 35 in max). Without vae_rand_b (min) the noise is the between-seed spread of vae_rand and RQ0 (a) and the noise check of RQ1 are skipped; without vae_rand — no RQ2 (1), RQ3 and C3; without seam-processing variants — no RQ3 (b, c) and C4. Without TRTR (real, regime R) there is no transfer ratio (RQ4, RQ5).
    • Fidelity against three references: Config.fidelity_extra_refs = ["train", "oot"] (default). Tables without @oot metrics give the ranking and RQ4 without the OOT slice.
    • Run configuration: hypotheses.run takes it from artifacts/<profile>/tables/config.json (written by the collect stage).
    • At least 3 seeds for per-dataset tests; 10 are planned (minimal p ≈ 0.001).
    • At least 3 datasets for global tests; 9 are planned.
    • A timing table from a single hardware type for RQ6.

    Custom methods, tests and hypotheses¶

    • A method (bench.methods.register_method, EXTENDING.md) enters the ranking and RQ0–RQ9 automatically; its family is determined from its description.
    • A statistical test: hypotheses.stats.register_test(name, level="dataset" | "global") — the test's columns are added to all comparison tables.
    • A hypothesis about a specific method: hypotheses.catalog.register_hypothesis("CS16", "title") — appears in the report together with the case study.
    • Case-study roles: the method (--case-method), the noise pair (--noise-pair), baselines (--baselines), the task of the case-study cells (--case-task, by default Config.stitch_task).
