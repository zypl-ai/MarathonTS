    Datasets for the experiments¶

    Open datasets on which the methods from BASELINES.md are compared by the metrics from METRICS.md. Licences are listed in DATASET_LICENSES.md.

    Principle: the same datasets for all tasks¶

    All utility tasks (U2.1–U2.9) are built from a single target series of a dataset, so every dataset below is used in all 14 tasks (nine task groups U2.1–U2.9) — there is one set of datasets for the whole experiment. To this end, for all datasets:

    • a single target column is chosen (channel 0 of the pipeline);
    • the step is brought to one hour (aggregation by mean or sum), the calendar is the hour of week (BENCHMARK.md, Protocol: data);
    • the length after aggregation is ≥ 17,000 hours: with a 60 / 20 / 20 split (train / val / OOT, profile max, the full series) and T = 168 this gives K ≥ 60 windows, enough for h = 168 and for events with q = 0.99. Profiles min and mid take the last 10,000 hourly points of every dataset with a 50 / 25 / 25 split: 9,664 points after the embargoes, train 5,000, val = OOT = 2,332, K = 30 (artifacts/run_h200/tables/datasets.parquet).

    Tasks (from METRICS.md, U2)¶

    ID Task Type
    U2.1 Upper tail: change over h = 24 above the 0.90 quantile binary classification
    U2.2 The same at horizons h ∈ binary classification
    U2.3 Rare events: q ∈ binary classification (strong imbalance)
    U2.4 Lower tail: change below the 0.10 quantile binary classification
    U2.5 Down / neutral / up multiclass classification (3 classes)
    U2.6 Value x[t+h], h ∈ regression
    U2.7 Quantiles 0.1 / 0.5 / 0.9 at horizon h = 24 quantile regression
    U2.8 Vector x[t+1 : t+24] multi-step regression
    U2.9 std over the next 24 hours regression (volatility)

    Every dataset below is used in all tasks U2.1–U2.9.

    Descriptions¶

    1. ETTh1 — Electricity Transformer Temperature¶

    Loads and oil temperature of a power transformer in China, July 2016 – June 2018, 1-hour step. Columns: date, six loads (HUFL, HULL, MUFL, MULL, LUFL, LULL) and the oil temperature OT. A standard long-horizon forecasting benchmark (Informer, Autoformer, etc.), with no missing values. The shortest in the set — the lower bound on length.

    • Source: github.com/zhouhaoyi/ETDataset, file ETT-small/ETTh1.csv
    • Paper: Zhou H. et al. Informer: Beyond efficient transformer for long sequence time-series forecasting. AAAI 2021

    2. Electricity Load Diagrams 2011–2014¶

    Electricity consumption of 370 clients in Portugal, 2011–2014, kW per 15 minutes. The first column is the time, the rest are clients; separator ;, decimal comma. Clients connected later have zeros at the start. The target series is the total load aggregated to hours: strong daily and weekly seasonality.

    • Source: UCI #321

    • Author: Trindade A., 2015

    • Leakage for Chronos: the Chronos training corpus contains this dataset (Electricity 15 min, hourly and weekly, all 370 clients, without only the last 24 points; Ansari et al., Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series, TMLR 2024, Table 3). The benchmark's target is the sum of the clients, so Chronos has seen the val and OOT periods of this series in aggregated form; its results on electricity are not out-of-sample.

    3. Individual Household Electric Power Consumption¶

    Consumption of a single household in France, December 2006 – November 2010, 1-minute step. Columns: Date, Time, active and reactive power, voltage, current, three sub-meters. About 1.25% of the rows are missing (marked ?). The target series is the hourly mean of Global_active_power. A noisy series with sharp peaks — a test on irregular dynamics.

    • Source: UCI #235
    • Authors: Hébrail G., Bérard A., 2012
    • Leakage for TimesFM: the TimesFM 3.0 training data (GiftEvalPretrain) include this dataset as sceaux (from BuildingsBench): one hourly series, 2007-01-01 – 2010-11-26, equal to the hourly mean of Global_active_power on every common hour after a 1-hour label shift. TimesFM is left out on household (Config.leaked).

    4. PJM Hourly Energy Consumption (PJME)¶

    Hourly consumption of the PJM East region (USA), 2002–2018, megawatts. The set also contains ~12 files for other PJM regions in the same format (Datetime, <REGION>_MW). The longest series in the set (largest K). There are isolated duplicates and gaps at the transitions to daylight saving time.

    • Source: kaggle.com/datasets/robikscube/hourly-energy-consumption
    • Primary data: PJM Interconnection LLC

    5. Beijing PM2.5¶

    PM2.5 concentration at the US Embassy in Beijing and airport weather, 2010–2014, 1-hour step. Columns: No, year, month, day, hour, pm2.5, DEWP, TEMP, PRES, cbwd, Iws, Is, Ir. pm2.5 has about 2000 missing values. A heavy right tail and smog episodes (tail tasks U2.1–U2.4).

    • Source: UCI #381
    • Paper: Liang X. et al. Assessing Beijing's PM2.5 pollution: severity, weather impact, APEC and winter heating. Proc. Royal Society A 471, 2015
    • Leakage for TimesFM: the TimesFM 3.0 training data (GiftEvalPretrain, built on LOTSA) include "Beijing Air Quality" (UCI #501: PM2.5 and weather of 12 Beijing stations, March 2013 – February 2017). It is another file, but the same city, pollutant and hours as this series over 2013–2014 — the period of the min window. TimesFM is left out on beijing_pm25 (Config.leaked).
    • Leakage for Chronos: the Chronos training corpus includes KDD Cup 2018 (air quality of Beijing and London stations, January 2017 – March 2018; Benchmark I of Ansari et al., 2024). Same city and pollutant, other stations and years: a related pair, so Chronos is left out on beijing_pm25 too.

    6. Jena Climate 2009–2016¶

    Weather station of the Max Planck Institute for Biogeochemistry (Jena), 2009–2016, 10-minute step, 14 variables: pressure, temperature, humidity, wind, etc. The target series is temperature averaged to hours. Strong daily and yearly seasonality with weak weekly seasonality — a check that the "hour of week" calendar does not impose spurious structure.

    • Source: Max Planck BGC Jena; ready-made file — jena_climate_2009_2016.csv.zip (Keras / TensorFlow)

    7. Metro Interstate Traffic Volume¶

    Hourly westbound traffic on I-94 (Minneapolis – St Paul), October 2012 – September 2018, plus weather and holidays. Columns: holiday, temp, rain_1h, snow_1h, clouds_all, weather_main, weather_description, date_time, traffic_volume. There are duplicate hours (several weather descriptions) and a large gap in 2014–2015. The duplicate hours are averaged, and the series starts after the last gap longer than 30 days (June 2015). Very strong weekly structure.

    • Source: UCI #492
    • Author: Hogue J., 2019

    8. Bike Sharing (hourly)¶

    Hourly number of Capital Bikeshare bicycle rentals (Washington, D.C.), 2011–2012, with weather and calendar. Columns of hour.csv: instant, dteday, season, yr, mnth, hr, holiday, weekday, workingday, weathersit, temp, atemp, hum, windspeed, casual, registered, cnt. Several hours are missing (zero demand) and are set to 0. Count data with a trend between years.

    • Source: UCI #275
    • Paper: Fanaee-T H., Gama J. Event labeling combining ensemble detectors and background knowledge. Progress in Artificial Intelligence 2, 2014

    9. Bitcoin Historical Data¶

    Minute-level OHLCV data for BTC/USD (Bitstamp), from 2012 to the present. Columns: Timestamp, Open, High, Low, Close, Volume. Before 2017 many minutes have no trades; the series is taken from bitcoin_start = 2017-01-01 to bitcoin_end = 2026-10-01 (exclusive). The target series is the log of the hourly close price. A financial series: weak seasonality, volatility clustering, heavy-tailed increments.

    • Source: kaggle.com/datasets/mczielinski/bitcoin-historical-data
    • Leakage for TimesFM: GiftEvalPretrain includes the Monash "Bitcoin" set (18 daily series, among them the Bitcoin price, 2009 – mid-2021). The same asset at a daily resolution: a related pair, so TimesFM is left out on bitcoin in every profile — also in min, whose window (2025–2026) lies after it (Config.leaked).

    Leakage into pretrained models¶

    A dataset whose object is in a pretrained model's training data is not out-of-sample for it: the method is left out on that dataset in every ranking, question and case-study test (Config.leaked, bench.config.LEAKED); the other methods keep every dataset. The analysis always applies the list of the current code (not the copy saved with the tables), so a pair added later takes effect on the next run of notebook 7 without recomputing anything; --leaked replaces it from the command line.

    Rule. Left out are both - exact pairs: the same data, the same source series, in the training corpus; - related pairs: the same object — the same dataset, the same city and pollutant, the same asset — at other stations, years or resolution, whether or not the periods overlap with the benchmark window.

    Kept are pairs that share only the phenomenon (bike rentals in another city, gridded weather reanalysis) and datasets that a model uses only for evaluation.

    The audit (October 2026) checked every model × dataset pair against the primary sources: Chronos — the paper's Appendix B, Table 3 (pretraining = pretraining-only sets + Benchmark I; Benchmark II is zero-shot evaluation only); TimesFM 3.0 — the model card and the full list of GiftEvalPretrain sub-datasets (Hugging Face Salesforce/GiftEvalPretrain, LOTSA builders of uni2ts), with the candidate sub-datasets downloaded and compared with the benchmark's raw files; Timestral — the model card and the release note.

    How the datasets differ (why there are nine)¶

    Property Datasets
    Strong weekly seasonality Electricity, PJME, Metro, Bike
    Strong daily, weak weekly Jena, ETTh1
    Heavy tails, spikes PM2.5, Household, Bitcoin
    Volatility clustering, weak seasonality Bitcoin
    Trend / level drift Bike, ETTh1, Bitcoin
    Gaps and duplicates requiring preprocessing Household, PM2.5, Metro, Bike, PJME
    Short (K ≈ 60) ETTh1, Bike
    Long (K ≥ 250) PJME, Jena, Bitcoin
